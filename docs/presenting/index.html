Preparation

the day before, then 30 minutes before

A smooth demo is decided before it starts. Load clean data, open the right windows, and rehearse the order-to-cash story once.

The day before

Do

On the demo copy only, reload a clean demo company so every record in this script exists and is in the state described:

php artisan migrate:fresh --seed
php artisan db:seed --class=DemoDataSeeder
npm run build
php artisan optimize:clear
Careful. migrate:fresh deletes everything in the database it points at. Check DB_DATABASE in .env first; never run it on a customer's real database.
Check

30 minutes before

Set up
  • Window A (normal browser window): sign in as manager@demo.test.
  • Window B (private or incognito window): sign in as sales@demo.test. Two windows keep two people signed in at once. Every demo user's password is Demo@12345.
  • Theme Light for a projector, browser zoom 100–110%, window at least 1440 pixels wide. Line tables on order and PTC pages get cramped on narrower screens.
  • Pin tabs in Window A: Dashboard, SO-26-0002, PTC-26-0003, DN-26-0003, INV-26-0002, Reports › Trial balance.
  • Turn off notifications and close unrelated apps and tabs.
  • Have the project guide link ready to share at the end.
Check

The demo company, in one breath

Know

Demo Sports Industries in Sialkot makes boxing gloves (10, 12 and 16 oz), goalkeeper gloves and match footballs. Export customers: Northfield Sports (USD), Rheinland Sportartikel (EUR), Albion Fight Gear (GBP), Gulf Athletics (AED); local customer Lahore Sports Mart (PKR). Vendors supply leather, trims and ball components; one contractor stitches footballs. Seven staff are on payroll, two of them paid by piece.

1 · Opening

0:00 – 0:05no screen yet, or the login page

Set expectations: why we are here, what they will see, how long it takes, and how questions work.

Say

"Thank you for your time. Today I'll show you IProduction, the system that will run your orders, purchasing, production, stock, deliveries, accounts and payroll in one place."

"We'll follow one real export order through the whole factory, from the customer's order to the money in the bank, and then look at the office side: stock, finance, payroll and reports. It takes about an hour and a half."

"Please stop me any time. If a question needs a longer answer, I'll note it and we'll come back to it at the end."

Ask

Go round the room: name, role, and the one thing that would make their day easier. Write these down and refer back to them during the demo.

Point out
Bridge

"Before I click anything, one minute on how the system thinks. It makes everything after it easy to follow."

2 · The big picture

0:05 – 0:10talk, or point at the sidebar

Give them the three ideas that explain every screen, so the demo feels predictable instead of overwhelming.

Say

"Everything that happens in your business is a document: an order, a goods receipt, a production card, a delivery, an invoice, a payment."

"Every document has the same life. It starts as a Draft you can change freely. A manager Approves it, which locks it. When it's Posted, the system updates stock and the accounts by itself, in one step that either fully succeeds or changes nothing. A posted document is never edited; mistakes are corrected with a return, a credit note or an adjustment, so the history is always true."

"Your export customers keep their own currency. A dollar customer is invoiced and paid in dollars. Rupees only appear when you convert the money at the bank and type in what the bank actually credited."

Point out
Bridge

"Let's sign in and see what a manager sees first thing in the morning."

3 · First look

0:10 – 0:20Window A, manager

Make them comfortable with the screen layout, so they recognise every page that follows.

Sign-in and dashboard manager@demo.test

Show
  • The sign-in page: email or username and password. Mention that two-factor authentication can be switched on per user in Profile.
  • The Dashboard. Walk the tiles left to right: open orders, late orders, stock shortages, production by stage, rejected stock, pending approvals, cash and bank, foreign balances, receivables, payables, this month's deliveries, top customers.
  • Click Open on one tile to show that every number leads to its detail.
Say

"This is the manager's morning view. Pending approvals shows what's waiting for you; stock shortages shows orders that nothing is covering yet. Each person sees the tiles for their own work."

Moving around

Show
  • The sidebar groups open and close, and remember their state. The current page is highlighted.
  • The theme menu at the top right: Light, Dim, Dark, or System to follow the computer.
  • Sales › Sales Orders: press / to jump to search, type Northfield, then use the status filter, the date range and a column sort. Click Export to show Excel and CSV.
Say

"Every list in the system works the same way: search, filters, dates, sorting and export to Excel. Learn it once, use it everywhere."

Anatomy of a document SO-26-0002

Show
  • Open SO-26-0002 (Rheinland, EUR, Partly Delivered).
  • The top: number, customer, date, status badge, and the buttons this document allows now (Plan, Order cost, Print).
  • The lines: ordered, reserved, on PO, in production, delivered and invoiced per line, with hints such as "Ready to deliver".
  • The side panels: details, linked supply, attachments, notes and the full History of who did what and when.
  • Print, then Download PDF on the printout.
Point out
Bridge

"Now let's follow an order through the whole factory, the way your team will use it every day."

4 · Order to cash

0:20 – 0:55Windows A and B; other users where noted

The heart of the demo. Seven scenes in the order the work really happens. Create a little live to show it's real, then open the prepared records to show each later stage without waiting.

Scene 1 · Taking an order Window B · sales@demo.test

Show
  1. Sales › Sales Orders › New sales order.
  2. Customer: Gulf Athletics Trading LLC. The currency fills in as AED and cannot be changed.
  3. Add two lines: Match Football, 120 pcs at 58; Goalkeeper Glove, 50 pairs at 64. Leave tax empty.
  4. Press Ctrl + S. The order gets the next number, for example SO-26-0008, as a Draft.
  5. Point at the top: there is no Approve button for a sales user.
Say

"Your sales team enters the order exactly as the customer sent it, in the customer's currency. Tax is per line and empty by default, so export orders carry no tax. The number comes automatically. And notice that sales can't approve their own order."

Point out

Scene 2 · Approving Window A · manager

Show
  1. Refresh the Dashboard: Pending approvals now counts the new order.
  2. Open it and click Approve. The badge changes to Approved and the History records who approved it and when.
  3. Mention Return to draft (needs a reason) and Cancel order (needs a reason, and only while nothing has happened against it).
Say

"Approval locks the order. From here on, any change leaves a trace with a reason. Which documents need approval at all is your choice, per document type, in Settings."

Point out
Bridge

"The order is confirmed. The next question every factory asks: where will the goods come from?"

Scene 3 · Planning supply Window A · manager

Show
  1. Sales › Planning. Walk the columns: required, free, reserved, on order, in production, shortfall, and the next action for each line.
  2. Rheinland's SO-26-0002 line shows 48 footballs free in stock. Explain Reserve available free stock; then show the line moving to Reserved.
  3. Show Create draft purchase orders and New contractor job as the other ways to cover a shortfall, and the Plan button on an order.
Say

"Planning answers 'can we ship this?' line by line. Free stock can be reserved so nobody else takes it. Anything missing becomes a purchase order or production, linked to this order. Orders on the way count as incoming, never as stock, so you never promise goods you don't have."

Point out
Bridge

"Let's see what happens when we buy the materials."

Scene 4 · Buying and receiving Window A · manager

Show
  1. Purchasing › Purchase Orders › PO-26-0003 (Ball Components, Partly Received). Mention Mark as sent and Receive goods.
  2. Open its receipt GRN-26-0003: 10,000 panels received, 9,900 accepted, 100 rejected and returned. Each accepted line became a lot with its own cost.
  3. Purchasing › Vendor Bills › BILL-26-0002 (Partly Paid), then Finance › Payments to show a payment settling bills.
Say

"The store records what actually arrived: received, accepted and rejected. Rejected goods sent back never enter your stock. Accepted goods enter as a lot with its cost, and the vendor's balance goes up at the same moment. A small extra above the order is accepted within your tolerance; anything more needs a manager's decision."

Point out
Bridge

"Materials are in. Now the part no generic accounting software can do: tracking work on your shop floor."

Scene 5 · Production on the floor Window A · manager

Show
  1. Production › PTCs › PTC-26-0003: 150 pairs of 10 oz gloves for Northfield's order.
  2. The stage board: Cutting finished; Stitching 80 back and 70 still with Javed Hussain; Logo Embossing optional; Packing waiting.
  3. Open Record a transaction and read the types: material issue, extra issue, stage issue, stage receive, reject at stage, release to store, skip optional stage.
  4. Scroll to Material (expected vs issued), the BOM copy, the Timeline and Cost so far.
  5. Production › Contractor Jobs › CJ-26-0001: 300 footballs sent out, 250 back, material reconciliation.
  6. Briefly: Production › Wage Work (piece work waiting for approval) and Rejections.
Say

"Every batch has a card that follows it through its stages. At any moment you can see how many pieces are at each stage and who has them. Material, wages and contractor charges collect on the card as the work happens, so when the gloves are released to the store you know exactly what they cost. Rejected pieces are recorded with a reason, so you can see where quality is lost."

Optional live step. In Window B, sign out and sign in as production@demo.test. On PTC-26-0003, record a Stage Receive at Stitching from Javed Hussain for 70 pairs. Show the board update. Only do this if you rehearsed it.
Point out
Bridge

"The goods are made and set aside for the customer. Let's ship them."

Scene 6 · Packing and dispatch Window B · sales@demo.test

Show
  1. Sales › Deliveries › DN-26-0003 (Albion, 200 pairs, Packed).
  2. Explain the steps it went through: Allocate stock (the order's reserved lot), Add carton with weights, Packing complete.
  3. Print the Packing list and the Delivery note.
  4. Click Dispatch live. The status becomes Dispatched and the order's delivered quantity goes up.
Say

"The delivery takes exactly the lot that was set aside for this order. Cartons and weights produce the packing list for the shipment. When the truck leaves, Dispatch takes the goods out of stock and books their cost of sale, all in one click."

Point out
Bridge

"Shipped. Now let's get paid."

Scene 7 · Invoice, payment and returns Window A · manager

Show
  1. Sales › Invoices › INV-26-0002: Rheinland, EUR 4,184, no tax. The Balance panel shows EUR 2,010 still owed after receipt RCV-26-0003 and credit note CN-26-0001.
  2. Finance › Receipts › RCV-26-0001: Northfield's USD 7,900 invoice is cleared by USD 7,850 into the USD bank account plus USD 50 bank charges recorded as a deduction. RCV-26-0002 is an advance of USD 1,500 waiting for the next invoice.
  3. Finance › Conversions › CNV-26-0001: USD 5,000 converted, PKR 1,395,000 typed in as received.
  4. Sales › Returns › DR-26-0001: 6 pairs back; 4 restocked, 2 sent to rejections. Its credit note reduced the invoice.
  5. Finance › Ledgers: open Rheinland's statement in EUR.
Say

"Invoices are made from what was actually dispatched, so you can't bill twice or bill what you haven't shipped. Payments come in the customer's currency, bank charges included, and can be split across invoices; anything extra waits as an advance. Rupees appear only when you convert, at the rate your bank really gave you. Returns go back into stock or to rejections, and a credit note fixes the bill, never an edit."

Point out
Bridge

"That's the full journey of an order. Everything we did also updated stock and the books behind the scenes. Let's look at those."

6 · Control and safety

1:10 – 1:15Window B, then the Super Admin

Owners buy control. Make it visible.

Show
  • In Window B (sales): HR, Reports and Settings are missing from the menu, Finance shows only Ledgers, and there is no Approve button anywhere.
  • Open a posted document: no Edit button. Corrections go through returns, credit notes and adjustments.
  • Sign in as the Super Admin: Settings › Roles (permissions per role), Users, Numbering, approvals per document type, the period lock, and the Audit Log.
  • Profile: two-factor authentication.
Say

"Each person sees only their own work. Nothing posted can be edited or deleted, and every change is in the history with a name and a time. When you close a month, you lock it, and nothing can be posted into it any more. The database is backed up every night, and we can prove a backup restores before you go live."

Point out
Bridge

"So how do we get your business from today's spreadsheets into this?"

7 · Going live

1:15 – 1:20Super Admin

Make the move feel safe and concrete: a fixed path, with checks at each step.

Show
  • Settings › Opening Balances: the list of 12 types and a downloadable CSV template.
  • Describe the preview that flags every row with an error, and that a file with errors can't be posted.
  • Reports › Go-live › Cutover report: imported totals against your old system's totals.
Do not click Lock go-live in the demo. It permanently stops further opening documents and moves the period lock.
Say

"We pick a month end. You print your closing figures from today's system. We load your cash, stock, customer and vendor balances, staff loans, open orders and jobs on the floor from simple spreadsheets. The system checks every row, and the cutover report must match your figures before we lock. From that day, you work in IProduction."

Point out
Bridge

"Let me pull it together, and then it's your turn."

8 · Wrap-up and questions

1:20 – 1:30+stop sharing the app; show the Dashboard or nothing

Leave them with three clear messages, answer the parked questions, and agree the next step before anyone leaves.

Recap · 2 minutes

Say

"Three things to remember. One: one system from order to cash, and the books and stock update themselves as your people work. Two: you can see your shop floor live, every batch, every stage, every cost. Three: it's controlled. People see only their part, nothing posted can be changed silently, and every customer stays in their own currency."

Do

Tie back to the pain points you wrote down in the opening: "You mentioned … — that's the screen we saw in scene …".

Questions · 5 minutes or more

Do
  1. Answer the parking-lot questions first, in the order they were asked.
  2. Then open the floor: "What would you want to see again?"
  3. Use the prepared answers below. Show a screen if it helps.
  4. If you don't know, say so, write it down, and promise an answer by a specific day. Never guess about a feature.

Next steps and close · 3 minutes

Agree
  • A target go-live date (a month end).
  • Who sends the master lists: customers, vendors, items with variants, routes and BOMs, employees.
  • The list of users and their roles.
  • Training sessions per role, and a date to load opening balances.
Say

"I'll send you today's summary, the project guide and the list of open questions with answers. Thank you for your time."

Point out

If something goes wrong during the demo

Do
  • An error message on posting (for example a stock shortage): read it out. It is the system refusing to post something that isn't true, and nothing was changed. Then open a prepared record instead.
  • A button is missing: you're probably signed in as the wrong user. Check the name at the top right.
  • A page looks unstyled: run npm run build and php artisan optimize:clear during a break, then refresh.
  • You lose your place: come back to this sheet; the counter shows what's left.

Prepared answers

reference

Short, accurate answers to the questions customers ask most. Open one only when needed.

Can we run more than one company?

Each company gets its own installation and database, so data is never mixed. A second company is a second installation.

Where do exchange rates come from?

Customer documents don't use a rate at all; they stay in the customer's currency. PKR value is created only when you record a currency conversion and type in the PKR the bank actually credited.

Can we change an invoice after posting it?

No. Posted documents are locked. You issue a credit note, a return or an adjustment, so the history stays true and auditable. Drafts can be changed freely.

How is sales tax handled?

Tax is an optional rate per invoice line and defaults to none, so export invoices carry no tax. When tax applies, it posts to Taxes Payable.

Does it work on a phone or tablet? Offline?

It runs in any modern browser and the layout adapts to smaller screens. There is no separate mobile app, and it needs a connection to the server.

Who can see salaries, costs and profit?

Only roles given those specific permissions: salary, cost, profit and bank balance each have their own. Roles are edited in Settings › Roles.

Can we require approval for some documents and not others?

Yes. Approval is switched on or off per document type in Settings.

How do we know who changed what?

Every document shows its full History, and the Audit Log in Settings shows all changes across the system with the user and time. Cancelling and overriding always require a reason.

What about backups?

The database is backed up automatically every night and old backups are pruned after a set number of days. A command restores the latest backup into a scratch database and checks it, to prove backups work before go-live. Copies should also be kept off the server.

Can we get our data out?

Every list exports to Excel or CSV, and every report prints, saves as PDF and exports to Excel.

How do we bring in our existing balances and open orders?

Through Settings › Opening Balances: twelve types, each a CSV template, checked row by row, compared with your old totals in the Cutover report, then locked.

Can we change document numbers?

Yes. Each document type's prefix and next number are set in Settings › Numbering.

Can you add a report we need?

New reports can be added to the Reports area, and they get printing, export and permissions automatically. Note the request with an example of the figures they want.

Can we stop people posting into a closed month?

Yes. Set the period lock date after closing a month; nothing can be posted on or before it.
